# Nourish: neighborhood edition

The primary job is to turn a food search into a feasible, remembered visit. The visual identity should feel like a welcoming neighborhood food cooperative, with the precision of a useful city guide.

## Design decisions before implementation

- **Palette:** white paper #FAFBF7; forest #244D3E; near-black ink #192C24; cabbage #DEE998; muted blue #DDE8EB; harvest yellow #E7BD64. Green belongs to actions and navigation; cabbage gives the public-facing brand a recognizable physical character. Status colors communicate meaning.
- **Type:** Outfit for distinctive, generous headlines; DM Sans for readable interface text. Sentence case, no italic headline fragments, no decorative all-caps eyebrows. Practical information stays at a comfortable 13–16px.
- **Layout:** a neighborhood atlas, built around a single search action and an image of food changing hands. Asymmetric home composition, a large practical directory, quieter supporting sections. Use flat rows for comparable results and structured panels for tasks. Avoid chopping all content into matching little cards.
- **Motion:** a restrained parallax image treatment, one coordinated introduction, and scroll-driven progress through the actual find–travel–collect journey. Fade-ins occur at meaningful section changes. Interactive controls and essential search results are never hidden behind an animation. Respect reduced motion and small-screen performance.
- **Icon discipline:** keep the brand mark, transport choices, map/navigation controls, save state, and actionable status indicators. Remove icons added only to fill space, icon circles above headings, and arrows appended indiscriminately to every label.

## Critique of the first version

The previous cream-and-italic headline, repetitive rounded cards, multiple little floating badges, tracked uppercase labels, and tiny supporting text could describe nearly any generated startup site. The new identity comes from New York neighborhoods, a legible atlas, the material color of produce crates, and a trip that visibly progresses toward food collection. Photography supports the shared meal, not a fictional impact statistic.

The interface must make source quality, unconfirmed schedules, and actual routing status easy to understand. No visual polish may make an illustrative service appear real.
